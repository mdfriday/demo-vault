# What Is a Digital Garden

A digital garden is a public collection of notes that are allowed to be imperfect, interlinked, and revised — more greenhouse than museum.

I use the phrase carefully. It is not a synonym for "messy blog." It is a publishing stance: growth in public, links over chronology, and revision without shame.

## Properties I care about

- **Links over chronology** — WikiLinks like `[[MDFriday]]` matter more than the archive page
- **Growth in public** — seedlings are allowed
- **Ownership** — source files you can export, backup, and republish — [[Own Your Knowledge]]
- **Usefulness** — a garden should help a visitor *do* something, not only admire your vibe
- **Continuity** — notes can improve; old versions do not have to be embarrassing secrets

## How mine differs from a second brain dump

I separate **Journeys** (narrative of what happened) from **Assets** (methods you can take). That split keeps the garden from becoming either a diary or a cold wiki. Journeys carry dates and emotion. Assets carry frameworks and checklists. Both cross-link heavily.

The root is [[Now]] — who I am, what I'm doing, which Journeys are active — rather than a classic homepage of categories.

## Why this matters for MDFriday

If knowledge creators only ever see polished blogs, they will think publishing requires a persona. Gardens show another path: publish the thinking system itself. That is part of why I build [[MDFriday]] the way I do.

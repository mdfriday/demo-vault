# Why I Built My Digital Garden

I built this garden for three selfish-and-shared reasons:

1. **Dogfood** — if MDFriday is for knowledge creators, I should be one in public.
2. **Memory** — company learning evaporates unless it becomes notes with links.
3. **Invitation** — strangers can enter through [[Now]] and wander Journeys without needing my calendar.

A fourth reason appeared later: the garden is a product lab. Structure choices (Journeys vs category folders, Assets vs story notes) teach me what knowledge sites need. That learning feeds [[MDFriday Publish]].

## Why not only a blog

Blogs are optimized for episodes. Gardens are optimized for compounds. My [[One Person Company]] story is not a single post; it is a web: product, sales, architecture, ethos. A reverse-chronological feed would bury the Living Timeline under whatever I wrote last Tuesday.

## Design choices

- Journeys instead of taxonomy folders like `technical/` / `business/`
- Assets for playbooks others can reuse
- English-first bodies for global readers on sunwei.xyz
- Root [[Now]] page as a present-tense entrance, not a classic nav dump
- Heavy WikiLinks so Obsidian and the published site both feel alive

## What I hope visitors do

Not "consume my brand." I hope they steal methods from [[Assets]], understand why [[MDFriday]] exists, and maybe recognize their own publish pain in these notes. If they leave with one clearer decision about ownership — [[Own Your Knowledge]] — the garden did its job.

## Maintenance rule

If I cannot update [[Now]] monthly and add at least a few Journey notes per season, the garden is dying. A dead garden is worse than no garden — it signals abandoned thinking. Maintenance is part of the product thesis for [[MDFriday]].

# MDFriday Publish

**MDFriday Publish** is the product surface most people mean when they say they "tried MDFriday": select what should be public, preview, publish, and keep ownership of the Markdown.

## The job-to-be-done

*Help me turn notes I already trust into a site I can stand behind — without migrating my brain into another silo.*

That job is why the journey hub is [[MDFriday]], and why the strategy note [[Why Focus on Publish]] exists. Publish is the sharp edge of the company: everything else is either support for that edge or a distraction dressed as a platform dream.

## What good feels like

- I can publish a folder or a note without exporting my entire private vault.
- Preview is close enough to reality that I'm not surprised after deploy.
- Themes give me a grown-up look without a redesign project.
- Unpublish / history exist so mistakes are recoverable, not traumatic.
- WikiLinks and note-centric navigation still feel natural on the web side of the bridge.

## What I optimize for

Clarity over feature count. Local-first over convenience theater. Global-readable docs — [[Serving Global Users]] — over slangy insider copy. When I write release notes, I try to name the human moment they improve ("I can ship this essay without dragging three draft journals online") rather than listing internals.

## Relationship to Foundry

Publish is the cockpit. [[Building Foundry]] is the engine room. Users should feel the cockpit; builders (me) must understand both. The Hugo literacy story in [[Why I Read Hugo Source Code]] exists because themes and content models are not decorative — they are constraints the cockpit must respect.

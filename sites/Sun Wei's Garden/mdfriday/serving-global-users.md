# Serving Global Users

In September 2026 my default market assumption is **global**, not "China first then maybe overseas."

That is not a patriotic slogan and it is not a rejection of Chinese users. It is a product and distribution decision: knowledge creators who publish in English (or bilingual) and buy tools on international rails are a reachable market for a one-person company with an English site (sunwei.xyz) and a Markdown-native workflow.

## Why global now

1. **The pain is global** — Obsidian users, Hugo users, digital gardeners, indie consultants: the publish friction shows up in many countries.
2. **Distribution compounds in English** — forums, Reddit, HN, Docs, YouTube search — even when I speak Chinese with friends.
3. **One-person leverage** — I cannot run five localized GTM motions well. I can run one clear promise well. See [[How to Enter Global Market]].

## What changes in the product

- UI and docs bias toward clear English.
- Examples assume international hosting options (not one domestic CDN narrative).
- Pricing and packaging assume global payment norms as much as possible.
- Support tone stays human and concrete — screenshots > slogans.

## What does not change

Local-first. Markdown. Selective publish. Ownership. The ethos in [[Own Your Knowledge]] is not region-specific.

## Personal note

I am comfortable being bilingual in life and mostly English in public product writing. Short Chinese phrases appear where they fit my voice — especially on the [[Now]] page tagline — but the garden's main bodies stay English so global readers are not locked out.

## Metrics I care about

Not vanity geography heatmaps. Prefer: successful publishes by non-local users, support questions that reveal docs gaps, and whether English-first pages reduce repeated explanations in sales calls. Global is a distribution choice measured by outcomes, not by slogans.

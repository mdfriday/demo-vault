# Why Focus on Publish

Every founder wants to build a platform. Platforms are seductive: they promise lock-in, network effects, and a story that sounds big in a pitch.

I narrowed MDFriday to **publish** on purpose.

## Publish is the sharp edge

Knowledge creators already have an editor (often Obsidian). They already have notes. They already have opinions about structure. What they lack is a calm bridge from private thinking → public site.

If I try to also become their editor, their sync engine, their community, and their analytics suite on day one, I will build a mediocre everything.

Publish is the moment value becomes visible. It is also the moment trust is tested: *did you ship what I meant to ship, and only that?*

## What "focus" means in practice

- Prefer selective publish over "sync my whole life to the internet."
- Prefer static output and ownable hosting paths over a single rented silo.
- Prefer theme ecosystems that already exist (Hugo world) over inventing fashion from scratch.
- Prefer dogfooding — this garden is part of that — over imaginary personas.

See [[MDFriday Publish]] for the product surface, and [[MDFriday Technical Architecture]] for how the pieces fit.

## Tradeoffs I accept

Focus means saying no to adjacent features that would impress a comparison table. It means some users will need other tools for writing, sync, or collaboration. That is fine. MDFriday should be excellent at the last mile of knowledge → web.

## Business angle

A narrow promise is easier to sell. [[First 10 Paying Customers]] paid for publish, not for a vague "second brain cloud." The same focus helps [[Serving Global Users]]: English-first product language, clear job-to-be-done, fewer cultural assumptions about how people organize notes.

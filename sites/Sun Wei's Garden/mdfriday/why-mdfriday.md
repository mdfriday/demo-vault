# Why MDFriday

I didn't start MDFriday because the world needed "one more static site generator." The world already has excellent ones. I started it because **knowledge creators** — people whose competitive advantage is a growing body of notes — were stuck between two bad defaults.

On one side: keep everything private forever. On the other: migrate into a publishing stack that doesn't respect how they actually think (WikiLinks, daily notes, evergreen essays, half-finished seedlings).

I live in Obsidian. My company is a [[One Person Company]]. My public site should feel like an extension of the vault, not a second brain I have to babysit.

## The promise

**MDFriday** is publishing for knowledge creators: local-first Markdown → a site you control, with selective publish and a theme ecosystem that already exists in the Hugo world. The goal is not to replace your editor. The goal is to make "share this folder / this note" feel obvious.

That promise sits next to a stronger personal ethos: [[Own Your Knowledge]]. If your knowledge only lives inside a rented UI, you don't own the compound interest.

## What I refused to build

I refused a product that only works if you abandon local files. I refused a product that requires you to become a full-time DevOps person to ship a personal site. I also refused to frame the category as "attack the incumbents." The framing is positive: local-first publish for people who already care about their notes.

## Why the name stays MDFriday

Brand spelling matters. It is always **MDFriday** — never shouting-case variants. The name should feel like a calm Friday ship day for Markdown people, not a corporate acronym.

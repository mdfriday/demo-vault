# Finding My Business Model

A business model is not a slide. It is a repeatable story: who pays, why they pay, how you deliver, and how you don't drown.

## The model I converged on

**Product:** MDFriday — local-first publishing for knowledge creators.  
**Buyer:** people whose notes are part of their livelihood or serious craft.  
**Value metric:** time-to-public-site with ownership preserved.  
**Motion:** product-led with human conversations early; content and [[Building in Public]] as distribution.

The model is intentionally boring in the best way. I am not inventing a new category word every quarter. I am selling a clear job: publish from the notes you already trust. That clarity makes [[Sales]] conversations shorter and [[Marketing]] less theatrical.

## Rejected temptations

- Custom agency forever (income now, company never)
- Tiny life hacks app unrelated to my edge
- "Platform" dreams before publish worked
- Chasing every adjacent feature that would impress a comparison table
- Building a social network "for knowledge" because networks sound fundable

## Advantage

My advantage is not "I can code." Lots of people can code. The edge is the intersection: Obsidian-native workflows + static publishing literacy + willingness to sell. See [[How to Find Your Advantage]]. Without the sales willingness, the technical edge becomes a private hobby with nice Git commits.

## Global lens

English-first packaging and global payment assumptions — [[How to Enter Global Market]], [[Serving Global Users]]. A one-person company cannot run five GTM motions well. One clear promise, spoken in a language a global buyer can scan quickly, is a feature of the model, not an afterthought.

## How I know it is working

Not vanity traffic. Prefer: paid customers, repeated publish success, and objections that sound like product tickets. See [[How to Validate a Product]] and [[First 10 Paying Customers]].

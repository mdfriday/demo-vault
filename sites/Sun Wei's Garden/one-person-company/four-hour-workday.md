# Four Hour Workday

The four-hour workday is not a promise that MDFriday builds itself while I drink coffee. It is an experiment in **leverage**: can I design days where deep work + AI + automation produce more than a theatrical 12-hour grind?

I care about this because a [[One Person Company]] has no spare founder. If I burn out, the company pauses. If I work performatively long hours, I often produce less of what matters: clearer publish UX, better docs, better customer conversations.

## Rules of the experiment

- Protect a short block of deep builder focus
- Use AI aggressively for drafts, exploration, and chores — [[How to Use AI as a Builder]]
- Keep sales/marketing batched so they don't fragment every hour
- Measure shipped outcomes, not chair time
- Stop when the high-leverage block is done — do not fill the day with fake work to feel legitimate
- Allow exception weeks consciously (launches, incidents) without abandoning the default

## Why it matters

A one-person company dies by either underworking (no shipping) or overworking (no life). The ethos line is not decoration: Enjoy Your Life is part of the product of the company. Burnout does not make Foundry better. Exhaustion does not make publish UX kinder.

## What still takes longer than four hours

Support spikes. Launch weeks. Hard debugging. Relationship-building conversations that cannot be rushed. The point is not a rigid stopwatch religion. The point is a default design that assumes leverage, then allows exceptions with eyes open.

## How I judge success

Did [[MDFriday Publish]] get better? Did a customer succeed without me? Did a garden note capture a reusable lesson for [[Assets]]? Did I keep enough energy to be a person outside the work? If yes, the day worked — even if the clock looks unconventional to former-employer eyes.

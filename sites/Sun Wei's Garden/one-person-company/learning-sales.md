# Learning Sales

I used to treat sales as something other people did. Then I became a [[One Person Company]] and discovered sales is simply **structured empathy with a close**.

If nobody agrees to pay, you do not have a business model — you have a project. That sentence is blunt on purpose. It pushed me to learn.

## Skills I'm practicing

- Asking about the last time they tried to publish
- Repeating their words before pitching
- Offering a next step smaller than "believe in my vision"
- Hearing "no" as information
- Naming price without flinching, then shutting up long enough for the other person to think
- Qualifying fit early so I do not "win" the wrong customer

## Relationship to product

Objections become roadmap and docs. If five people stumble on selective publish, that is not a "messaging problem" only — it might be UX. Sales is a sensor for [[MDFriday]], not a separate sport you play after the product is "done."

Good sales conversations also protect focus. When buyers keep asking for CMS features, I can point back to [[Why Focus on Publish]] instead of shapeshifting weekly.

## Emotional work

The hard part is not scripts. The hard part is tolerating the silence after you ask for the sale, and tolerating the identity hit when someone is not a fit. Fit matters. Selling the wrong person creates refunds, bad stories, and a product pulled in ten directions.

## What "good" looks like for me

A conversation where the buyer leaves clearer — even if they do not buy. Notes that capture their words. A weekly habit of outreach that does not destroy deep work blocks (see [[Four Hour Workday]]). A calm link between sales learning and marketing learning — [[Learning Marketing]].

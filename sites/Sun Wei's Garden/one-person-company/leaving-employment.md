# Leaving Employment

Leaving was not a dramatic movie scene. It was a sequence of quieter admissions: I wanted to build something I could stand behind in public, and the paycheck was buying a version of safety that cost me momentum.

## What I prepared

- Runway math (boring, necessary)
- A product direction already itching — publish for knowledge creators
- A willingness to look incompetent in public while learning sales
- A note system that would become this garden, so learning would not evaporate into chat history

I also prepared psychologically for a dull truth: the first months would look like underemployment to people who measure worth by calendar density. That is fine. Density is not the same as progress.

## What I underestimated

How long identity lag lasts. For weeks you still answer questions as if you had a team badge. You don't. You have customers (maybe zero), a calendar, and a vault. You also underestimate how often you will want to rebuild the landing page instead of talking to a human — which is why [[Learning Sales]] had to become deliberate practice.

Another underestimate: how emotional "simple" admin feels when you are alone — taxes, invoices, support threads at odd hours. A [[One Person Company]] is romantic in essays and operational in Tuesdays.

## What helped

Writing things down in a garden instead of only in chat apps. Talking to potential users before polishing logos. Keeping the scope of [[MDFriday]] narrow — [[Why Focus on Publish]]. Using AI as leverage without pretending it replaces judgment — [[How to Use AI as a Builder]]. Returning to the ethos line when anxiety demanded busywork: Own Your Knowledge. Build Your Business. Enjoy Your Life.

## A note on timing

I did not wait for perfect confidence. Confidence is often a lagging indicator of reps. I waited for enough runway and a problem I was willing to stay with when the novelty faded. Publish for knowledge creators passed that test. Random app ideas did not.

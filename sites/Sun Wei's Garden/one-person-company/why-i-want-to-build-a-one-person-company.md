# Why I Want to Build a One Person Company

I want a company that fits a single accountable human — me — amplified by software and AI, not a theater troupe of roles I pretend to staff.

## What I mean by one-person

Not "I refuse help forever." Not "I hate collaboration." It means:

- I own the product thesis
- I own the customer promise
- I can ship without waiting for a committee
- Headcount is a last resort, not a status symbol

A one-person company can still buy services, partner, and hire contractors. The point is that the *company mind* stays coherent. When something breaks at 11pm, I know who is responsible. When something delights a user, I know which tradeoff made it possible.

## Why this shape

Employment taught me craft. It also taught me how easy it is to optimize for internal narratives instead of external value. I wanted a tighter feedback loop: talk to a user, change the product, see if money or gratitude shows up.

MDFriday became the concrete vehicle — [[Why MDFriday]] — but the company desire came first: agency over my time and my knowledge. I care about [[Own Your Knowledge]] as a personal rule and as a product principle. Building alone (with AI) forces me to keep both aligned.

## Constraints I chose

- Global market focus (Sep 2026): [[Serving Global Users]]
- Local-first product values instead of rented-only convenience
- Public learning: [[Building in Public]], this [[Digital Garden]]
- A work design experiment: [[Four Hour Workday]] — leverage over hustle theater

## What I am not optimizing for

I am not optimizing for looking busy. I am not optimizing for raising a story that requires a fifty-person org chart. I am optimizing for a durable product relationship with knowledge creators and a life that still has room outside the laptop.

# First 100 Subscribers

The first 100 subscribers are less a vanity medal and more a signal: a few strangers will let you interrupt their day again.

I do not confuse 100 subscribers with product-market fit. I do treat it as evidence that the channel is not only friends politely clicking. That is enough to keep investing carefully.

## What worked

- Specific demos related to publishing notes and gardens
- Titles that name the job-to-be-done instead of cryptic cleverness
- Cross-links from this garden and honest posts elsewhere
- Showing real UI, including rough edges, so trust can form
- Consistency with brand and promise — always **MDFriday** when the product appears

## What didn't

Random productivity chatter with no product spine. Overproduced intros that delayed the first useful minute. Posting on a schedule so aggressive that quality and shipping both collapsed.

## What I learned about audience

The viewers who matter ask questions that sound like customer objections. Those comments are gifts for [[Learning Sales]] and for docs. Applause comments are nice; actionable confusion is better.

## Numbers in context

One hundred is a threshold for courage, not a business plan. Revenue still comes from customers who publish — see [[First 10 Paying Customers]] — not from subscriber counts. The channel is a top-of-funnel amplifier for people who need to *see* the workflow.

## Next milestone mindset

I am not chasing a vanity ladder. The next milestone is better demos and clearer packaging for [[MDFriday]], plus notes that capture what viewers ask repeatedly. Growth without learning is just noise with a thumbnail.

# What I Learned From My First Videos

The first videos were awkward. That is fine. Awkward and shipped beats elegant and imaginary.

## Lessons

1. **Start with the problem**, not my biography. Viewers arrived for publish pain, not my origin story.
2. **Show the screen early** — knowledge creators want evidence.
3. **One video, one promise** — same discipline as [[Why Focus on Publish]].
4. **Imperfect audio beats unpublished wisdom.**
5. **Notes first** — outline in the garden, then record; the garden stays when the algorithm forgets.
6. **Say the product name cleanly** — **MDFriday**, every time.
7. **Stop recording when the demo is clear** — extra takes often add anxiety, not clarity.
8. **Invite a next step** — a garden link, a docs page, a question — without a hard-sell monologue.

## Process I use now

Write a short outline as a note. Decide the single job of the video. Record the demo path once or twice. Cut ruthlessly. Publish. Then capture leftover insights back into Journey notes or [[Assets]].

## Craft note

Lighting and microphones matter eventually; honesty matters immediately. Viewers forgive DIY production if the demo respects their time. They do not forgive ten minutes of throat-clearing before the first useful frame.

## Tooling stance

I use the lightest editing path that keeps the demo intelligible. Fancy transitions are optional. Accurate cursor movement on the publish flow is not. If AI helps me outline or clean transcripts, I still own the final cut — [[How to Use AI as a Builder]].

Those lessons sound small. Together they are how a channel stays useful to a product company instead of becoming a second full-time job.

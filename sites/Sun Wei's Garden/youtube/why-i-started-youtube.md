# Why I Started YouTube

I started YouTube because some ideas are easier to *show* than to describe: publish flows, vault structure, the difference between a garden and a blog dump, the moment selective publish saves you from shipping a private journal by accident.

Text is still my home — this [[Digital Garden]] is durable. Video is a spotlight. I use it when motion and interface timing matter.

## Goals

- Teach in public without needing a perfect studio
- Create artifacts that compound search traffic for [[MDFriday]]
- Force clarity — if I can't demo it, the UX may still be muddy
- Support [[Building in Public]] with receipts, not only claims
- Practice speaking about the product in clear English for global viewers — [[Serving Global Users]]

## Non-goals

Becoming a full-time creator who no longer ships product. Chasing trends unrelated to knowledge publishing. Building a second job that cannibalizes [[Four Hour Workday]] leverage.

## How I choose topics

If a note in the garden would be clearer with a screen recording, it is a candidate. If the only hook is my face reacting to industry drama, it is not. The product spine stays visible: [[MDFriday Publish]], local-first ownership, one-person company lessons.

## Early expectations

I expected awkwardness and slow growth. I did not expect video to replace writing. If YouTube ever competes with shipping [[MDFriday]], shipping wins. The channel exists to serve the company and the craft, not to become the company.

## Success criteria

A video succeeds if a stranger can follow the demo and if it leaves a durable link back into the garden or product docs. Subscriber count is a trailing indicator. Clarity is the leading one.

# Headline Variations and Swipe Files

## What it is

Two practice habits from the course that improve headline quality over time:

1. **Model the best in the business.** Find someone in your niche who writes excellent, clickable titles, study their examples, and adapt them to your own topic. The course points to blogger Jeff Bullas as an example and breaks down why his titles work: specific, a number, and a clear outcome ("12 new plugins that will boost your online sales"), or the common-mistake pattern ("5 fundamental flaws with your brand messaging and ways to fix them").
2. **Practice with many variations.** "Copywriters aren't born. They practice." The course recommends writing a headline **25 times** for a blog post, with at least **10 variations** for every piece of content, even an email, and then choosing the one that grabs attention most. It mentions free "25 headlines" worksheet tools with built-in length checkers and templates.

A **swipe file** is the standard name for the collection that results from habit 1: saved examples of headlines (and other copy) that worked, used as templates.

## Why it matters

The first headline you write is rarely the best. Your first ideas are the obvious ones, and better angles appear after you've used those up. Modeling gives you proven structures, so you're not guessing. Since the headline gets far more readers than the body (see [[magnetic-headlines|Magnetic Headlines]]), the extra ten minutes spent on variations is usually the best use of your writing time.

Use this every time you title a post, video, email or landing page. It matters most for pieces you'll promote.

## How to use it

**Inputs:** a swipe file, the piece's promise, customer phrases ([[write-like-your-customers-talk|Write Like Your Customers Talk]]), and a timer. **Output:** 10–25 variations, a chosen headline, and two or three alternates to test.

**Building the swipe file:**

1. Pick two to five sources in or near your niche whose titles consistently make *you* want to click (blogs, YouTube channels, newsletters, top posts in your communities).
2. Save headlines that stand out into one note, with the source and a tag for the pattern (number, how-to, mistakes, question, specific outcome).
3. For each saved headline, write one line on *why* it works (specific number, concrete outcome, curiosity).
4. Also save your own top-performing headlines, with their results.

**Writing variations:**

1. Write the promise in one sentence.
2. Set a timer and write **at least 10** (ideally 25) headlines. Go through the patterns: number, how-to / even if / without, mistakes, question, five Ws, keyword first ([[search-friendly-titles|Search-Friendly Titles]]).
3. Adapt swipe-file structures: keep the structure and replace the topic and outcome with yours. Never copy a headline word for word.
4. Check length for the channel.
5. Shortlist three, read them as the target reader, pick one, and keep the other two for testing.

AI can speed up step 2 ([[ai-assisted-writing|Writing Faster with AI]]), but you still do the choosing.

> Swipe-file structure: "[Number] [tools/ways] that will [outcome] in [timeframe]". Adapted: "3 ways to publish your Obsidian notes this weekend". Another: "[Number] mistakes with [topic] and how to fix them" becomes "4 mistakes that make publishing Obsidian notes painful (and fixes)". Illustrative only.

## How to verify it worked

- **Variation win rate:** when you test your chosen headline against the alternates (email split tests, title tests where platforms support them, or republishing), how often does the one you picked actually win? Improving judgment shows up here.
- **Average click or open rate over time:** track the average for your headlines by month. The practice is working if the trend rises.
- **Pattern learning:** your log should reveal which structures work best with your audience.
- **Speed:** writing 10 variations should get faster with practice while quality holds.

**Good:** your first choices increasingly match the test winners, and your average click rate improves. **Bad:** all variations are tiny rewordings of one idea. Push for different angles, not synonyms.

## How to keep optimizing

- **Add to the swipe file weekly** and prune examples that no longer feel fresh.
- **Write variations in different directions:** benefit-led, pain-led, curiosity-led, specific-outcome-led.
- **Study your losers.** Write down why a headline underperformed.
- **Model outside your niche too.** Structures often transfer well across topics.
- **Review your log quarterly** and turn your best patterns into your own templates.

## Common mistakes

- Writing one headline and publishing.
- Copying other people's headlines word for word instead of adapting the structure.
- Variations that differ by one word rather than by angle.
- Choosing the headline you like best instead of the one the reader needs.
- Never testing, so you never learn which choices were right.

## Checklist

- [ ] Swipe file exists, tagged by pattern, with "why it works" notes
- [ ] At least 10 variations written for this piece
- [ ] Variations cover different angles and patterns
- [ ] Length checked for the channel
- [ ] Chosen headline plus alternates saved for testing
- [ ] Results logged back into the swipe file

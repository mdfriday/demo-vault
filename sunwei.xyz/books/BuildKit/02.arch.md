# The veil of the architecture

Another Friday morning, bright sun.
Yuan Xiaobai walked into a meeting room with whiteboards on all four walls and a large television in the middle, and confirmed the time on the meeting system as he always did.
He always liked to arrive five minutes early, to prepare the room and what he was going to share.
Today was exactly one year after he graduated. He was long used to the company and its atmosphere.

He opened the computer and looked at the Buildkit research notes he had prepared. It made him a little nervous.
This was the weekly meeting. By habit, everyone would continue the topic left from last time: research the Moby Buildkit architecture. For him, reading the source of a large open-source project like this was the first time. One week was only enough to look up and understand how to study source code. He had not reached the real source yet. That made him somewhat nervous.

Then two colleagues walked in. One was Long Fei, a mainstay of the team.
The other was Jia Dazhi, who had started this interest group.
They arrived right on time, not early and not late.

As soon as he came in, Long Fei greeted Yuan Xiaobai: Xiaobai, early again. Let me show everyone the good thing I prepared. As he spoke he was already sitting nearest the door, sharing his screen.

Hey, what's the rush. Today is Xiaobai's one-year anniversary. Let's congratulate him first.
Jia Dazhi hurried to remind him.

Ha, if you hadn't said it I would have forgotten.
Long Fei rubbed his head, a little embarrassed, then immediately, brightly, congratulated Yuan Xiaobai.

Everyone laughed their way into today's topic: sharing the Moby Buildkit architecture.

The television in the room had long been showing Long Fei's result, the Moby Buildkit flow chart:

![buildctl-build.jpg](./img/buildctl-build.jpg)

Looking at a flow chart so long that one screen could not show it all, Yuan Xiaobai froze.
Everyone had started from the same place. None of them had experience with Go projects. How had Long Fei read the source so quickly, and sorted the flow chart out? Hard to imagine.
From the introductions to reading source that he had looked at this week, he had learned there really is a method called main-line reading. According to the flow you want to understand, you quickly sort out the call flow of the code, so the rough relationships are clear.
Like making friends: get a rough picture first, then go into the details.
But this method is hard, because the reader has to connect what they learned before, especially the knowledge of the programming language.

There is another one: problem-driven reading. You usually read the source with a clear purpose, such as fixing some ISSUE, then opening a PR (Pull Request).
This method can reach your own goal efficiently.
But it is a bit different from what we need this time. We need to understand the overall structure, and fill in the domain knowledge, so we are ready for the next stage, a PoC (Prove of Concept).

Yuan Xiaobai's curiosity was hooked at once. No time to think it through. He asked: Long Fei, how did you sort out the main-flow sequence diagram in just one week?

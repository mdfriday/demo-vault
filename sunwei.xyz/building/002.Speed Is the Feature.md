# 001. Speed Is the Feature

*Sep 28–Oct 04, 2026*

**Current stage goal**: Find the first 10 international users who genuinely need MDFriday.

## What I expected

At the beginning of the week, I thought my job was to make MDFriday more capable.

I spent most of my time shipping new Digital Garden themes, improving the free plan, and discussing roadmap items with users.

I assumed that users would mainly evaluate MDFriday based on features.

## What actually happened

This week I shipped two new Digital Garden themes and simplified the free plan.

Those changes were useful, but they were not the most important thing that happened.

The biggest insight came from talking with users and showing demos.

Again and again, I noticed the same pattern.

When I explained features, users were interested.

When I showed a note being published in less than 10 seconds, users immediately understood the value.

No additional explanation was needed.

The reaction was often:

> "Wait, that's it?"

That moment taught me something important.

Users don't care about the publishing process.

They care about reaching the result.

The faster they get there, the easier it is for them to understand why the product exists.

## What I learned

### 1. Speed is not a technical metric. It's a product feature.

I used to think publishing speed was an implementation detail.

Now I think it's one of the core product features.

The journey from:

Plugin Installed → Published Website

is the first experience every user has with MDFriday.

Every second spent waiting weakens that experience.

Every step removed strengthens it.

This week confirmed that our strongest demonstration is not a feature comparison.

It's showing someone a successful publish in under 10 seconds.

That is the clearest expression of the product's value.

### 2. Every comparison is a learning opportunity

Many users naturally compare MDFriday with Quartz, Digital Garden, Obsidian Publish, and other publishing solutions.

At first, I viewed these comparisons as competition.

Now I see them as research.

Users are essentially telling me:

> "Help me understand the landscape."

Instead of avoiding the comparison, I think we should embrace it.

A useful next step is creating a continuously updated guide covering:

- What publishing options exist today
- The strengths of each approach
- The trade-offs involved
- Which type of user each tool serves best

The goal is not to convince everyone to choose MDFriday.

The goal is to help people make informed decisions.

If the information is useful and honest, trust follows naturally.

### 3. Users want outcomes, not infrastructure

This week I also spent time systematically learning SEO.

The more I studied it, the more I realized how much hidden complexity exists behind a successful website.

Most creators don't want to think about:

- Sitemap generation
- Metadata
- Structured data
- Search indexing
- Technical SEO
- Analytics
- Conversion funnels

They want to create content.

Everything else is a distraction.

That reinforces the long-term direction of MDFriday.

The mission is not simply publishing Markdown.

The mission is helping people turn knowledge into digital assets.

Publishing is only one step.

Discovery, growth, and operation matter too.

## What changed my mind

I started the week thinking feature expansion was the primary path forward.

I ended the week believing that reducing time-to-value is more important.

The strongest signal wasn't a new theme.

It wasn't a new pricing model.

It wasn't a roadmap item.

It was seeing people instantly understand the product after watching a 10-second publish.

That experience clarified the direction.

If we can make publishing feel effortless, users will naturally explore everything else.

## What I'm doing next

Three priorities emerged from this week.

### 1. Continue talking to users

The goal remains unchanged:

Find the first 10 international users who genuinely need MDFriday.

Most of the useful insights are still coming from conversations.

### 2. Optimize speed

Focus on:

- Faster builds
- Faster publishing
- Fewer steps
- Lower activation friction

The benchmark is simple:

Publishing should feel almost instant.

### 3. Build trust through education

Create a public guide comparing publishing options such as:

- Quartz
- Digital Garden
- Obsidian Publish
- MDFriday

Not as marketing material.

As a genuine resource for the community.

Helping users understand the space is one of the fastest ways to build credibility.

## Weekly Success Criteria

- [x] 10+ user conversations
- [x] 1 useful release: Added two new Digital Garden themes
- [x] 1 demo: Demonstrated sub-10-second publishing
- [x] 2 community discussions
- [x] 1 building note: this one
- [x] 1 documented learning: Speed is the feature

## Key Takeaway

This week taught me that the fastest way to explain a product is not with words.

It's with a result.

A user can forget a feature list.

A user can ignore a roadmap.

But when they see a note become a website in less than 10 seconds, they immediately understand what makes the product different.

That's the direction I'm doubling down on.

# 1. Customer Research & Buyer Problem Identification

## What is This?

Customer research is the process of clarifying BEFORE marketing begins: **Who is most likely to buy, what are their biggest pain points, and what results do they want to achieve?**

Without this step, your website pages, headlines, emails, and SEO can easily become self-talk that nobody understands.

## KNOW | Foundation

### Why It Matters

- It determines WHAT you sell, not what you "want to sell"
- It determines HOW you speak so users feel "you truly understand me"
- It reduces wasted effort and prevents creating content/products nobody cares about

### You're Looking for "Hungry" Demand, Not Just Any Audience

The course emphasizes that truly high-value markets typically show three signals:

1. **Users have a specific problem** - Not imagined, but repeatedly mentioned
2. **Users want to solve it urgently** - It bothers them, wastes their time, or costs them money
3. **Users are willing to pay for solutions** - They see value in fixing this problem

## UNDERSTAND | Deep Dive

### The Goal is Identifying "Convertible Problems"

Not every problem is worth marketing. A problem is worth pursuing if it meets three criteria:

- **Is it real?** - Multiple users mention it, not just your imagination
- **Does it have emotional intensity?** - Does it create anxiety, friction, wasted time, or lost opportunities?
- **Is it unsolved?** - If existing solutions are already mature and cheap, it's harder to differentiate

### Output: Customer Language, Not Abstract Labels

Your most valuable material isn't abstract tags. It's the exact words customers use:

- "My biggest problem is..."
- "If this was solved, I could..."
- "I tried this before, but it never worked..."

These direct quotes will feed into:
- Homepage copy
- Headlines  
- Email subject lines
- CTA text
- SEO content angles

## MASTER | Execution

### Practical Steps

1. Write out your assumed buyer persona
2. List their 3-5 most common problems
3. Assess which relate to money, time, efficiency, identity, or risk
4. Prioritize researching problems that are "painful, urgent, and have willingness to pay"
5. Collect direct customer quotes and build your own language library

### Four Key Information Categories to Track

- **Pain Points** - Where things hurt, feel inefficient, or waste time/money
- **Desires** - What does their ideal outcome look like?
- **Obstacles** - Why haven't they achieved it yet?
- **Previous Solutions** - What have they tried? Why didn't it work?

### Knowledge Checklist

- [ ] Do I know who my core users are?
- [ ] Do I know their 3 most urgent problems?
- [ ] Do I understand their ideal desired outcome?
- [ ] Do I have direct customer quotes, not just my assumptions?
- [ ] Do I know which problems indicate willingness to pay?

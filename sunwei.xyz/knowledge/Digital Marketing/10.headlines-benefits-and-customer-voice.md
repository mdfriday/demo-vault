## What is This?

The most direct influence on clicks and reading engagement: whether headlines attract attention, whether content focuses on user benefits, and whether the tone sounds like customers talking to themselves.

## KNOW | Foundation

### Why Headlines Matter

Classic insight from the course: **More people read headlines than read body copy.**

So headlines determine whether your content gets consumed at all.

### Three Core Principles

1. **Headlines grab attention first**
2. **Benefits outsell features**
3. **Customer language outperforms marketing jargon**

## UNDERSTAND | Deep Dive

### Common Powerful Headline Patterns

- **Number-based**: "7 Trends..." or "11 Mistakes..."
- **How-to**: "How to..."
- **Error-focused**: "X Mistakes You Might Be Making"
- **Question-based**: "Are You..."
- **Keyword-first** (for SEO): Key term upfront
- **Specific results**: "Boost by 600%"

### Why "Benefits" Outsell "Features"

- Features describe product attributes
- Benefits describe user transformation

Example:
- **Feature**: 1GB storage
- **Benefit**: 1000 songs in your pocket

Users naturally understand and want results, not specs.

### Why Customer Language Wins

When users see familiar expressions in your copy:
- They feel understood
- They perceive relevance
- They build trust

Pulled directly from customer interviews, natural language outperforms polished marketing-speak.

## MASTER | Execution

### Process for Writing Headlines

1. Define the page goal
2. Identify the outcome users want
3. Write 10 different angles/versions
4. Verify each is specific, concrete, and understandable
5. For SEO titles, place keywords early

### The "So What?" Exercise

Write any feature, then repeatedly ask yourself "So what?" until you reach user-felt benefit:
- "What does this mean?"
- "What's the benefit to users?"
- "Why would they care?"

Keep asking until you reach something users actually feel.

### Knowledge Checklist

- [ ] Is my headline specific, not vague?
- [ ] Does it convey benefit, results, or curiosity?
- [ ] Do I have 5-10 headline variations?
- [ ] Have I translated features into user benefits?
- [ ] Am I using customer-familiar language patterns?

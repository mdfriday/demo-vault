# 14. Keyword Research Strategy

## What is This?

Keyword research isn't finding "the highest volume words." It's finding: **How your target users search, whether those terms are worth pursuing, and whether you have a chance to rank.**

## KNOW | Foundation

### Three Goals of Keyword Research

1. Find how users actually express their needs (their language)
2. Assess whether these keywords have business value
3. Choose keywords you have realistic opportunity to rank for

### Course Core Perspective

- Think like a buyer, not an industry insider
- Don't chase massive head terms immediately
- Start with body and long-tail keywords
- Build keyword combinations rather than betting everything on one term

## UNDERSTAND | Deep Dive

### Why Long-Tail Keywords Are Better Starting Points

Head keywords typically have:
- High search volume
- Extreme competition
- Long-established sites permanently ranking

Long-tail keywords, while smaller volume:
- Are more specific and concrete
- Align closer to actual user problems
- Have stronger purchase intent
- Offer more differentiation opportunity

### Keywords Map to Purchase Funnel Stages

Different keywords serve different stages:

- **Awareness**: "What is..." / "How does..."
- **Research**: "Best..." / "Comparison..." / "Review..." / "How to..."
- **Purchase**: "Pricing..." / "Tool..." / "Course..." / "Service..."

Further down the funnel, stronger commercial intent.

### Research Three Key Metrics

- **Search volume** - Are people actually searching?
- **CPC/Commercial intent** - Is this worth pursuing?
- **Competition strength** - Do you have a realistic chance?

## MASTER | Execution

### Practical Process

1. Start from customer problems—list core topics
2. Use Wikipedia / Google autocomplete / related searches to expand keywords
3. Use keyword tools to assess volume and CPC
4. Initial filter: identify concrete long-tail variations
5. Manually search each term—assess first-page competition strength
6. Choose keywords that are relevant, valuable, AND winnable

### Keyword Selection Principles

Prioritize keywords that:
- Align with your product/content
- Serve a specific problem
- Have clear user intent
- Have manageable competition
- Lead to subscriptions, consultations, or sales

### Knowledge Checklist

- [ ] Do I start from user problems, not industry jargon?
- [ ] Have I collected a broad set of long-tail keywords?
- [ ] Have I considered both search volume and commercial intent?
- [ ] Have I manually reviewed search results for competition?
- [ ] Do I have a keyword portfolio, not all bets on one term?

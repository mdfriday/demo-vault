## What is This?

AIDA is a classic copywriting framework that walks users through each stage—from noticing content to taking action.

AIDA stands for:
- **A**ttention - Grab their notice
- **I**nterest - Keep them engaged
- **D**esire - Make them want it
- **A**ction - Push them to act

## KNOW | Foundation

### When AIDA Works

Nearly all marketing content can use this structure:
- Emails
- Sales pages
- Blog posts
- Advertisements
- Video scripts
- Social media copy

### The Problem It Solves

Much content is written but goes nowhere. AIDA provides "forward momentum"—it pulls readers down the page step-by-step toward action.

## UNDERSTAND | Deep Dive

### 1. Attention: Make Them Stop

This is usually the headline, subject line, or opening statement.

**Core question**: Why should users pay attention to THIS content over everything else competing for their time?

### 2. Interest: Keep Them Reading

After they click, if the first paragraph bores them, they leave.

At this stage, you're signaling:
- This is relevant to them
- This offers something new
- This deserves continued attention

### 3. Desire: Transform Information Into Want

This step converts "product info" into "user aspiration."

Common tactics:
- Show benefits and outcomes
- Use real-world examples or case studies
- Add social proof, numbers, evidence

### 4. Action: Make the Next Step Clear

Finally, explicitly tell users:
- What to do next
- Why now is the right time
- What happens after they take action

## MASTER | Execution

### Universal AIDA Template

- **Attention**: Open with results, contrast, numbers, or pain points
- **Interest**: Explain why this problem matters
- **Desire**: Paint the ideal outcome, add evidence and proof
- **Action**: Close with a clear CTA

### Four Diagnostic Questions

1. Have I grabbed attention?
2. Have I maintained interest in the opening?
3. Have I turned information into desire through benefits and proof?
4. Have I made the next step crystal clear?

### Knowledge Checklist

- [ ] Does the headline truly carry the Attention stage?
- [ ] Does the opening quickly establish Interest?
- [ ] Does the middle create Desire through benefits, not just features?
- [ ] Does the ending include a clear Action?
- [ ] Does the overall content feel like it's moving forward?

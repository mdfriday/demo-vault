# Writing Faster with AI: Draft, Then Improve

## What it is

AI text tools can produce a usable first draft of almost any marketing text from a plain-language request: a blog post, a tweet thread, a polite reply to a review, a cold email. The course shows this with OpenAI's playground and makes one key point: **"it's always easier to improve text instead of writing from scratch."** AI gives you the draft. You make it good.

The course's examples of what to ask for:

- "Write me a blog post about email marketing for beginners."
- "Write me a tweet thread about the best practices when buying a domain."
- "Write a polite response to the following review:" followed by the review
- "Write a cold email selling web development services."

It also notes that many paid AI writing tools are built on the same kind of models and add templates, multiple languages, multiple variations and tone-of-voice settings.

## Why it matters

A blank page is the main bottleneck for a solo marketer. AI removes it. Use it for:

- first drafts of posts, emails and landing-page sections
- **many variations** of headlines and CTAs (see [[headline-variations-and-swipe-files|Headline Variations and Swipe Files]])
- tidy, spell-checked replies to comments and reviews
- templates to adapt, such as outreach emails

It won't give you what matters most: real customer insight and your own point of view. Those still come from [[customer-pain-points-and-desires|research]].

## How to use it

**Inputs:** a clear request, your research (pain points, desires, exact customer quotes), the structure you want, and a tone. **Output:** an edited, fact-checked piece in your voice.

1. **Decide the job first.** What is the piece, who is it for, and what single action should the reader take? Begin with the end in mind (see [[call-to-action|Call to Action]]).
2. **Give the AI context, not only a topic.** Instead of "write a landing page", say: "Write a landing page for [audience] who struggle with [pain, in their words] and want [desire]. Use the AIDA structure. End with the CTA [action]. Plain, friendly tone."
3. **Ask for a structure.** Ask for [[aida-formula|AIDA]], a numbered list, or "10 headline options". Structured requests give more usable drafts.
4. **Generate several versions** and pick the strongest parts.
5. **Improve it by hand:**
   - Replace generic phrases with real customer language ([[write-like-your-customers-talk|Write Like Your Customers Talk]]).
   - Turn features into benefits ([[features-into-benefits|Flip Features into Benefits]]).
   - Check every fact, number and claim. Delete anything you can't back up.
   - Add your own experience, examples and opinions.
6. **Read it aloud** as the target customer. Cut anything that sounds like filler.

> Prompt: "Draft a short Reddit post for Obsidian users who want to publish a folder of notes as a website without learning a static site generator. Lead with the problem in plain words, show a quick before/after, ask for honest feedback, no hype." Then rewrite the draft with real quotes from user conversations and the founder's own voice, and follow the subreddit's self-promotion rules. Illustration only.

## How to verify it worked

- **Time saved:** time from idea to published piece, compared with writing from scratch. Track it for a few weeks.
- **Quality held or improved:** compare the performance of AI-assisted pieces with your earlier pieces on the same channel (open rate for emails, click rate for headlines, replies or comments for posts).
- **Edit depth:** if you publish drafts almost untouched, check for generic tone. Readers notice. If you rewrite everything, your prompts need more context.
- **Accuracy:** zero unverified claims in published text. Any factual error is a failure, whatever the speed gain.
- **Voice check:** would a regular reader recognize it as yours?

**Good:** faster output, stable or better engagement, a recognizable voice. **Bad:** more content but fewer replies, or complaints that it "sounds like AI".

## How to keep optimizing

- **Build a prompt library.** Save prompts that work, with your audience description and customer quotes built in.
- **Feed it your best examples.** Paste a past piece that performed well and ask for the same style.
- **Use AI for variations, you for selection.** Generate many headlines or CTAs, then test the best few.
- **Review tools occasionally.** The course's own walkthrough (a free account and a playground) reflects the tools of its time. Interfaces, pricing and models change, so re-check what works now instead of following old steps.
- **Keep a "never" list** of phrases, claims and styles to strip from every draft.

## Common mistakes

- Publishing the first draft without editing.
- Prompting with a topic only, so you get generic text.
- Letting AI invent statistics, testimonials or features.
- Using AI instead of customer research rather than alongside it.
- Replying to customers with obviously templated text that ignores their specifics.

## Checklist

- [ ] Goal, audience and CTA decided before prompting
- [ ] Customer language and pain points included in the prompt
- [ ] Structure requested (AIDA, list, variations)
- [ ] Draft edited for voice, benefits and specificity
- [ ] Every claim checked
- [ ] Performance compared with earlier pieces

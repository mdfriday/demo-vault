# Digital Marketing

Knowledge points from the **Digital Marketing Strategy** course (instructor: Daragh). Each article covers one idea at mastery level: what it is, why it matters, how to use it, how to check that it worked, how to keep improving it, common mistakes, and a checklist.

## About the course

The course covers the whole digital marketing stack: market research, setting up a website, email marketing, copywriting, SEO, YouTube, social platforms, Google Ads and Google Analytics, and app marketing. It is designed to be followed in order: **research first, then a home base, then traffic and sales**. Each channel section also works on its own.

The copy of the course in my notes currently contains only the course overview, the market research section and the copywriting section. So this set covers those parts. More articles will be added as more lectures are captured.

> [!tip] Why this matters for me right now
> At the current stage (finding the first 10 international users) the most useful parts are market research and copywriting. They help me understand the people I'm building for and describe MDFriday in their words. The MDFriday examples in each article are illustrations, not results.

## Articles in learning order

### 1. Course overview

- [[research-first-marketing-sequence|Research First: The Digital Marketing Build Order]]: understand the customer, build a home base, then drive traffic, and always in that order.

### 2. Market research

- [[customer-pain-points-and-desires|Pain Points and Desires]]: the foundation of all marketing. Know where customers are, where they want to be, and build the bridge.
- [[three-question-market-research-survey|The Three-Question Market Research Survey]]: a free, short survey that finds problems, desired outcomes and unmet needs.
- [[unsolved-problem-filter|The Unsolved Problem Filter]]: use "how difficult has it been to solve?" to find real market gaps.
- [[pre-launch-email-list|Build an Email List Before You Have a Product]]: turn research respondents into an early list, segmented by pain.
- [[four-customer-research-methods|Four Ways to Uncover Pain Points and Desires]]: surveys, social listening, review mining and interviews.

### 3. AI-assisted writing

- [[ai-assisted-writing|Writing Faster with AI: Draft, Then Improve]]: let AI beat the blank page, then improve the draft with research and your own voice.

### 4. Copywriting

- [[aida-formula|The AIDA Formula]]: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action, the default structure for persuasive content.
- [[magnetic-headlines|Magnetic Headlines]]: numbers, how-to, mistakes, questions, the five Ws, specificity and images.
- [[search-friendly-titles|Search-Friendly Titles]]: keyword first, punctuation, and a length that doesn't get cut off.
- [[headline-variations-and-swipe-files|Headline Variations and Swipe Files]]: model the best writers and write 10–25 variations before choosing.
- [[features-into-benefits|Flip Features into Benefits]]: use the "So what?" test to turn what the product does into what the customer can do.
- [[call-to-action|Writing an Irresistible Call to Action]]: one clear, verb-led, visible next step in every piece.
- [[urgency-in-copy|Honest Urgency]]: real deadlines and limits that turn intent into action.
- [[risk-reversal|Risk Reversal]]: guarantees, no-card trials and small time commitments that make "yes" easy.
- [[write-like-your-customers-talk|Write Like Your Customers Talk]]: copywriting's one big rule. Reuse customers' own words about pain and desire.

## Suggested path

If you only have an hour: start with [[customer-pain-points-and-desires|Pain Points and Desires]], then [[three-question-market-research-survey|the survey]], then [[write-like-your-customers-talk|Write Like Your Customers Talk]] and [[aida-formula|AIDA]]. Together they take you from understanding a customer to writing a message that speaks to them.

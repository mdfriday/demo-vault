# Access Control & Collaboration

← [[mdfriday/voice-of-custom/index|Voice of the Customer]]

## What users are saying

This theme doesn't come up often, but it shows the clearest willingness to pay. **On the reader side**: one password for the whole site isn't enough. People want page-level or per-user passwords, members-only subscription areas, and SSO / company-account login. They want a public landing page plus a private area, because a site-wide password makes a community knowledge base impossible to discover. Some only want students, family or a specific team to see the content. **On the author side**: teams want several people to maintain one site, with separate read-only and edit permissions, review before publishing, and without paying extra for every collaborator.

> [!info] Signal (approximate)
> Reader access control: ~20 VOC cards · ~6 YouTube comments (plus 2 asking for collaborative editing).
> Author collaboration: ~7 web supplement sources.
> Ranked **#13**: low frequency, high willingness to pay.

## Why it hurts

- **A lot of knowledge isn't meant for everyone.** Course materials, client documents, team SOPs and family records all need "only these people".
- **All-or-nothing permission models aren't enough.** A site-wide password is either fully on or fully off; a collaborator can either change everything or nothing.
- **Per-seat pricing scares teams away.** Every extra collaborator means another subscription, and users feel "charged at every layer" (see [[pricing-and-lock-in|Pricing & Lock-in]]).

## How people cope today

- A site-wide password shared privately, while worrying that it will leak.
- Moving to collaboration platforms such as Notion, GitBook or ClickUp.
- For single notes, Share Note's optional encryption; see [[single-note-sharing|Single-note Sharing]].
- Chinese enterprise users want viewers to sign in with a company Microsoft account; see [[chinese-community-voices|Chinese Community Voices]].

## MDFriday's response

- Access control and multi-person collaboration are not among the currently confirmed capabilities. Password protection, encrypted sharing, revoke at any time, teams / multiple authors: **all (unconfirmed)** (an earlier showcase draft mentioned "encrypted sharing, revoke at any time"; verify before promoting it).
- **Selective publish** covers part of the need: content you don't want public simply isn't published and stays local.
- If a team plan comes later, "priced per site, collaborators free" would directly answer the complaint that every collaborator has to pay separately.

## Open questions

- Which scenario shows the strongest willingness to pay: courses / member content, internal team wikis, or client documentation?
- Could "public landing page + private area" be a differentiator for the Personal plan?
- This need is closely tied to MDFriday's positioning ("knowledge creators grow, sell, and serve"). Is it worth running user interviews on it early?

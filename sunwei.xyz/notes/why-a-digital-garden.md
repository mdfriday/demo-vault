## Core idea

Digital gardens grew out of a simple claim: **you need to own your intellectual property, and let that knowledge keep growing.**

- A blog is a timeline: once written, a post sinks.
- A digital garden is a map: notes link to each other and keep being revised.

## Why it suits me

My approach is build in public: let doing replace talking, and honestly document the work of building a one-person company.

What this process produces isn't pieces of content. It's assets:

- One user conversation → one learning
- One learning → one product decision
- One product decision → one building note
- Several building notes → one knowledge note

One real event produces several assets. A digital garden is the structure that can hold that.

## How I do it

- I write in Obsidian. The notes are the source files and belong to me.
- I publish this folder as a website with [MDFriday](https://mdfriday.com). This site is itself a real case, and it is the founder's site.

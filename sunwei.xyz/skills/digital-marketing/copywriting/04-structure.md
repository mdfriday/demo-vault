Use the skeleton for the piece type from [[01-job|the job]]. Do not merge two skeletons. Do not add a section you cannot fill from the facts. Leave it out and say so in the log.

The order under each skeleton is AIDA: attention is the headline you already picked, interest is the opening, desire is the middle, action is the close.

## Article (default)

1. Headline.
2. Opening paragraph. Their situation, or one specific fact from the inputs. Not a definition of the industry.
3. The point, in one or two sentences: what they will be able to do.
4. Middle. Three to five sections. Each has a subheading and either a step or a benefit. One idea per section.
5. Proof. Only quotes, numbers, and cases from the facts. No proof section if you have none.
6. Objections. Only ones the user listed, or ones you can answer from the facts.
7. The action. See [[06-action|the action]].

## Email

1. Subject line (the headline).
2. Preview line. A second hook, not a repeat of the subject.
3. Opening line. A hook or their situation. Use their name only if you were given a merge field, not a real person's name.
4. Body. One story or one lesson.
5. One sentence on why it matters to them.
6. One action, with the link placeholder `[link]` if no URL was given.
7. Signature. Use the name and site you were given. Do not invent them.

## Product description

1. Headline. Benefit, not the product category.
2. Subheading. One specific limit or objection ("even if …"), only if it is true.
3. The problem, in their words.
4. The offer, in one sentence.
5. Three to five benefits. Each one follows [[05-body|feature to outcome]].
6. Proof, if you have it.
7. What is included. Features live here, after the benefits, each still tied to an outcome.
8. Questions you can answer from the facts.
9. The risk reversal, only if a real guarantee or a real cost-to-try was given.
10. The action.

## Sales page

Use this longer order only for a sales page:

1. Headline
2. Subheading
3. Problem
4. One-line hook, true
5. Story, only if you were given one. Do not write a fake founder story.
6. The offer
7. Benefits
8. What is included
9. Proof
10. Objections
11. Why act now — only a real deadline or a real limit
12. Guarantee, only if given
13. The action, repeated three to five times down the page
14. A P.S. that restates the offer and the real limit, not a new promise

Long pages are for sales pages, where the reader still has objections. An article stays at three to five sections. Do not stretch an article to look like a sales page.

Then write the words in [[05-body|the body]].

# Copywriting checklist (distilled from the knowledge articles)

## AIDA
- [ ] Decide the single action before writing
- [ ] Attention: the headline answers "what's in it for me?"
- [ ] Interest: the first paragraph shows the reader's own situation or something fresh
- [ ] Desire: benefits, the life change, and real proof (verbatim quotes, not invented testimonials)
- [ ] Action: one visible CTA with an honest incentive
- [ ] Read stage by stage: would you open it from the headline? Keep reading after paragraph one?

## Headlines (see headline-swipe-file)
- [ ] Keyword first, 50–60 characters, one proven pattern
- [ ] Specific over clever; numbers match the body
- [ ] Written from 10+ variations, not the first idea

## Features into benefits
- [ ] Each feature bullet ends with "So you…" in the reader's terms
- [ ] Passes the "So what?" test: ask it until you reach a real-life outcome
- [ ] Benefits tied to the pains in the research, not generic ("save time")

## Write like your customers talk
- [ ] Reuse their exact phrases (from verified quotes) for pains and outcomes
- [ ] Plain words; no internal jargon or feature codenames
- [ ] Second person in the opening; founder first person in the story

## Call to action
- [ ] Starts with a verb; says exactly what happens next
- [ ] Smallest useful first step (one note, five minutes, no account if possible)
- [ ] Only one primary CTA per post

## Urgency (honest only)
- [ ] Use real deadlines only (a price change, a cohort date); otherwise skip urgency
- [ ] Never fake scarcity or countdowns

## Risk reversal
- [ ] State what it costs to try (time, money, data risk) and how to undo it
- [ ] State limits and temporary tiers plainly; the honesty is part of the pitch

What 17 Years of Software Development Taught Me

Lessons that stayed with me from engineering, networking, mobile delivery, team management and technology strategy — without turning them into slogans.

Thinking · Jan 18, 2026 · 10 min read

Seventeen years is long enough to forget what felt hard at the beginning, and short enough to remember that most progress came from doing the work — not from collecting titles.

Technology is a tool

Early on I cared about how things worked under the surface: JavaScript class systems, network protocols, mobile stacks. That curiosity still matters. But the useful question became: what problem does this solve for a real person?

Delivery teaches more than design decks

Leading delivery for clients and teams taught me that elegant architecture without shipping is incomplete. Presales, timelines, trust, and communication are part of the craft — whether we like that or not.

Strategy without building becomes fog

Later work around technology strategy and global collaboration expanded the view. It also showed a risk: talking about transformation is easier than changing systems. I want the next chapter to stay close to building.

Learn deeply. Build honestly. Find real problems. Create something useful.

What I'm doing with it now

I'm putting those experiences together into a one-person business experiment — using software, AI, and everything I've learned to turn knowledge into products. That's the story behind this site.

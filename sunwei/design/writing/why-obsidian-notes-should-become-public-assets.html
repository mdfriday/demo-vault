Why Obsidian Notes Should Become Public Assets

Personal knowledge is valuable — but only if it can leave your vault without forcing you to rebuild your whole workflow.

Tools · Mar 2, 2026 · 7 min read

Obsidian is excellent at holding thinking. Many people already have years of notes, links, and half-finished essays sitting in Markdown. The gap is usually not writing — it's publishing with ownership.

Private knowledge compounds slowly

Notes help you. Public assets help you and other people, and they create a surface for feedback, trust, and products. A digital garden, a knowledge site, or a focused library can become something you own and build on.

Don't make people change tools first

The publishing path should respect existing workflows: folders, front matter, wikilinks, and the habits people already have. If publishing requires a full migration, most knowledge never leaves the vault.

Your knowledge can become an asset — if you can publish it without starting over.

Where MDFriday sits

This is the problem MDFriday is exploring: publish Obsidian notes into websites and knowledge products people can maintain. Still early. Still learning from users.

A blog post is a finished object. You hold it until the outline closes, the examples behave, and you can imagine a stranger quoting the last paragraph. That is a good shape for an announcement. It is a bad shape for learning.

I learn by leaving the intermediate page up.

[[still-fuzzy]] is online because the contradiction is the thing I am trying to see. [[pothos-cuttings]] is online because that procedure has survived a season. They sit in different folders and wear different tags, and both are allowed on the site. The tag is the disclaimer. I do not need a second draft status in a CMS.

## What changes in public

When a seedling becomes budding, I do not move the file to a "published" folder. I add a tag. The URL stays. Backlinks from [[now/index|Now]] still resolve. Readers who subscribed get the next revision because the page is the same plant, watered.

## What I refuse to wait for

- A complete literature review before [[retrieval-as-light]] can exist
- A perfect calathea schedule before [[the-windowsill]] can exist
- A brand site that hides the fern named Draft

The work is the updating. Shipping the early note is how the updating stays visible.
